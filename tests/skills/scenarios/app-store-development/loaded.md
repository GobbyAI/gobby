Preserve the annotation in durable shared storage, show its staged status, and instruct the user to open the containing app. A documented user-initiated handoff may be possible, but must be verified for the exact caller, API, extension point and OS; native messaging success does not mean foreground launch. 2. **No. Choose permissions from the feature, and eliminate competing queue overwrites.** Apple says Safari extensions should limit website access to what functionality needs ([4.4.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). For the queue, a simple engineering option is immutable per-transfer files: write a unique temporary file, validate it, publish atomically, and assign clear consumption/deletion ownership. If both processes must mutate queue state, use transactional storage or interprocess locking. Atomic replacement alone still loses concurrent read-modify-write updates; a Swift actor coordinates only its own process. Verify signed App Group membership and simultaneous writes, reads, retries and deletion. Confidence: high on the race; actual API/permission support remains target-dependent. Apple's [archived shared-container guide](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/General/Conceptual/ExtensibilityPG/ExtensionScenarios.html) is historical support for synchronization, not current availability proof.
