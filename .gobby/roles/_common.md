# Rules for every role (read before your own file)

- Find your role file by your session in `roster.md`. Stay inside it. If work falls outside your role, send it to the Orchestrator; don't do it yourself.
- Title your session with your role. After you resolve your roster row, read your session with `gobby-sessions:get_session` and call `gobby-sessions:set_title` with your role file's heading (for example `Lane 1 developer (client chrome)`); gclient shows it after your session ref. Do this when `title_source` is not `manual`, or when the manual title begins with another session's ref (a predecessor's title carried into a successor). Leave any other manual title alone: someone wrote it by hand. When the Assistant tells you your role changed, retitle with the new heading.
- Load brevity at max (Josh, 2026-10-05: "make sure the common instructions tell agents to use brevity max"). Call `gobby-skills:get_skill(name="brevity", level="max")` at session start and after every compaction or clear, and apply it to every message and report.
- Cross-session messages go through `gobby-agents:send_message`. Never send keys into another session. Josh: "You don't interrupt lanes, you send messages to PD". The exception: the Assistant, the Orchestrator and Lane Managers may recover a stalled session with `gobby-sessions:send_keys`, resume keystrokes only, never steering, never into a credential prompt (Josh, 2026-10-08: "You and the orchestrator have standing permission to recover sessions using send_keys.").
- Do not spawn agents or launch pipelines. The automated task-close reviewer is the only permitted spawn path, and the Assistant, Orchestrator and Lane Managers may only resume an existing session with `spawn_agent` `resume_session_id`. The planning runbook launches the Writer, Enhancer, and Adversary as live seats.
- Developer lanes run on a claimed Gobby task, the task-close stop gate and session handoffs. Never start or resume native `/goal`, at kickoff or later (Josh, 2026-09-25). Report an inherited lane goal to the Orchestrator; only the Assistant, on Josh's instruction, clears it with `/goal clear` at an idle prompt. Keep the Gobby claim and handoff intact.
- Task metadata edits: the Assistant may call `gobby-tasks:update_task` when Josh asks, and the Orchestrator may call it as needed. Other seats route `update_task` requests to the Orchestrator. The `block-update-task` rule enforces this, even on a seat's own claimed task. Only the Assistant, Orchestrator, Analyst, Architect and Product Manager definitions exclude it; the last three write their discovery briefs into their assigned task. The Assistant's "when Josh asks" limit stays prompt guidance.
- A passing `close_task(preview=true)` checks deterministic gates; it is not reviewer admission. Lanes wait for an explicit Lane Manager release before `close_task(preview=false)`; that release alone admits the close, and no Orchestrator word is needed (Josh, 2026-09-30). If the service returns `close_review_busy`, wait for the active reviewer to finish and for Lane Manager release before retrying.
- Close backlog cap: 10 (Josh, 2026-10-08: "Reprioritize the GIL starvation fix, rule_engine latency if they're not being worked, and you can have a close queue of 10 before you need to drain one"). Earlier rule (Josh, 2026-10-05): "the backlog should never get more than 5 deep. if it does, the team will clear it before the lane managers assign new work." The backlog is every task, across all lanes, whose candidate was sent and which is not yet closed. Lane 7 planning tasks don't count toward it (Josh, 2026-10-05: "backlog cap: exempt Lane 7 planning"). Neither do tasks parked while they wait on a decision from Josh (Josh, 2026-10-05: "If you need to park a task because you're waiting on a decision from me, do so and put it in a Decisions needed: list during your 30 min report. Those tasks don't count against the close queue"). While it exceeds 10, no new task is assigned; assignment resumes as soon as it is 10 or fewer, with no drain to 0 (earlier rule, Josh, 2026-10-06: "Stop assigning new tasks when the close backlog > 5, no more wait until zero drain before new tasks"). Park any close that needs Josh's review, list it under `Decisions needed:` and keep the lanes moving (Josh, 2026-10-06: "park any closes that require my review and keep the lanes moving"). The Orchestrator orders the queue. Each Lane Manager keeps its lane moving and tells the Orchestrator what it needs to know to order it.
- No new task outside a lane (Josh, 2026-10-06: "the rule is no new task outside a lane."). Found work goes to the Orchestrator, which files it in the right lane. Existing tasks and epics outside the lanes stay where they are (Josh, 2026-10-06: "there are existing epics that don't occupy a lane").
- Only the Orchestrator creates worktrees (Josh, 2026-10-05). Each worker lane has one primary worktree. An extra worktree exists only for parallel work in that lane and gets its own developer and code reviewer; the Merge Manager merges it into the lane's primary worktree only, then cleans it up. Planners write in the main checkout on 0.5.0 and never use worktrees or clones; reviewers review in the developer's worktree and create none.
- Only Josh authorizes pushes, one exact SHA at a time. Josh: "Only I authorize pushes." The Orchestrator prepares a SHA that passes the pre-push checks, and the Assistant brings it to Josh. Once Josh authorizes that exact SHA, the Orchestrator runs `git push origin <sha>:refs/heads/0.5.0` (Josh to the Orchestrator: "When we push, you'll be the one to do it, you just have to get authorization from me."). No other seat pushes, keys a push or directs one.
- Only the Orchestrator (or someone Josh names) restarts the daemon. Every restart gets a global notice before it stops and a DAEMON BACK notice after it's healthy. The Assistant alerts Josh both times.
- Load has exactly one rule, Josh's: "Let's bump it to two consecutive >30" (Telegram, 2026-10-05; it was 24). The heavy-work hold starts only after two consecutive five-minute load readings above 30; one reading, or 30 exactly, triggers nothing. Heavy work resumes after two consecutive five-minute readings below 24 (Josh, 2026-10-02: "two 5m load measurements <24"). Focused runs in a seat's own worktree never wait for a Lane Manager release. No seat invents, lowers or changes a load threshold, admission limit or escalation level. Josh: "I didn't set that limit. Tell the LM it needs to ask first." Send any proposed change to Josh through the Assistant and wait for his OK. Josh has had to restate this five times (2026-10-02).
- Quiet hours are 04:45–06:45 CT for the Game Goblins jobs: no restarts or cutovers, and keep load low. Game Goblins jobs are never rerun.
- In anything Josh reads, every #NNNNN carries its title or a short phrase. Say "Systems nominal" when healthy. Give no load numbers unless load is breaching.
- When filing a task, combine it with closely aligned tasks rather than duplicating them (memory 8c1b9f80).
- Decisions reach Josh as buttons he can click, sent by the Assistant. Ask once and never nag.
