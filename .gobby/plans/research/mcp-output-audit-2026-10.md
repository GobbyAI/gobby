**Proxy envelope decision**

- Remove `response_time_ms` from agent-facing results entirely. Example: `"response_time_ms":344.2667920025997`. Rounding retains a field agents do not act on and saves less. Keep timing in telemetry and web-card metadata. #24539 explicitly requires the web card to retain timing; changing envelope recognition and timing transport belongs in that implementation.
- The September plan chose integer timing to retain the web card's numeric-envelope heuristic. Josh's newer October instruction and #24539 supersede that choice. Do not implement the older timing deliverable verbatim.
- Keep `success` and one `result` container for now. They distinguish failure from business payloads. Avoid broadly flattening envelopes: result-contract parity during the Rust migration needs an intentional, settled shape. No measured saving justifies that additional change here.
- Offload: keep `result_id`, retrievability, total size, a bounded preview and pagination/retrieval pointer. Move redundant `stored_chars` when equal to `total_chars`, `structure.keys`, scalar echoes and long repeated instructional prose behind full mode. Keep `stored_chars` whenever it differs, and never remove truncation/loss signals. Live S6 shows `count`/`query` both outside and inside preview, and a second copy of key names under `structure`. Estimated 100–250 tokens per envelope, variable by preview. Keep one short retrieval pointer. Full exact bytes remain retrievable.
- The wrapper exposed both MCP `content` and `structuredContent` to this research harness. That is not proof that each fleet provider sees both; do not claim a 50% fleet saving from deleting one without adapter-specific measurements.

### Risks

- Empty/default values can encode meaningful negative outcomes. Apply per-tool projections; never recursively delete every false/zero/empty value. Ownership, expiry, unsuccessful wake status, retry/failure signals and scope can change the next action.
- The October per-tool frequencies and raw audit attachment were unavailable, so the strongest aggregate totals are explicitly inherited from #24539. Smaller server estimates are sample-based, not fleet-wide forecasts. No new decision should depend on their exact rank.
- Mandatory skill/schema reloads dominate bytes but are policy, not removable metadata. Keep them outside the immediate cleanup. Existing September projections already specify much of the next tranche; reconcile that work rather than create duplicate implementation machinery.
- Rust result parity can accidentally preserve current waste. Freeze the cleaned contract first. R is a shared prerequisite, not evidence that a dedicated handler leaf already exists for every server.
